            The referee

            When a chancy moment is risked — a swing, a sneak, a press against someone — the house rules on it first, before the storyteller writes, and the ruling rides into the page as fact ("The house has ruled" on the receipt). Fights become real duels and battles with their own economy. The math is the house's; the words are the storyteller's. If the referee can't be reached, the story simply goes on unruled.

            A message saying #roll asks for a ruling on a quiet beat; #skip waves one off. Who is good at what lives in the drawer under "How they measure".

            How much the story remembers

            When a tale grows long, the oldest pages can't ride along word for word forever. The memory keeper folds what has scrolled past into short, faithful notes the storyteller still sees ("What remains" on the receipt). It works after each page is done, never during.

            Fewer pages kept word for word means smaller, quicker turns; more means the recent tale stays exactly as written for longer.

            The second reader only notes drift in the ledger ("Something drifted") and on the receipt — it never touches the words themselves.

            The thinking voice

            Some storytellers weigh the page aloud before writing it. The weighing rides its own channel and rests, folded, above the prose as "what the storyteller weighed". How hard the storyteller thinks is set on each connection above; here the story gets its own say, and you choose whether the weighing shows at all.

                How the tavern works

                One connection does everything. The address you add in Settings is both the storyteller and the house's quiet workers — the same key, the same device. Nothing is ever sent anywhere you didn't point it.

                A second connection can carry the workers. If you like, a cheaper or faster address in Settings → The workers takes over the background reading, so the story itself answers sooner. It's a frugality, not a need.

                What happens when you send a line:

                1. The house assembles what the storyteller sees — the frame, the craft, the brief, who's here, the state of things, what remains of older pages, and your words.
                2. The storyteller writes one answer, streaming as it comes. One generation per turn, always — nothing queues ahead of the story.
                3. Afterward, in the background, the workers read the finished page: the extractor keeps the ledger true, the scribe tends the character pages, the keeper folds old pages into memory notes, the second reader flags drift.

                The ledger (the drawer on the right) is what the house knows: the hour, who's here, who's hurt, what stands between people, what's happening elsewhere, what's true of them — and what the characters are like, where they are, and what they still owe. Every line of it is hand-editable and undoable.

                The rulebook holds standing instructions that wake when the scene calls for them — or when you pin them on.

                The frame and the note at the end are your standing words to the storyteller — one at the top, one slipped in last, before it writes.

                The receipt under any answer shows exactly what was sent that turn, slot by slot, with timings. The tavern keeps no secrets about what the storyteller saw.

                Your words live here. Everything — stories, keys, ledger, memories — sits in this browser's storage on this device. Backups are a single file you carry yourself (Settings → Backup).
